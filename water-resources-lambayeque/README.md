# Water Resources & Mesquite Forest Change — Lambayeque, Peru

During NASA DEVELOP, I helped extract and prepare satellite and environmental datasets for GIS analysis in Lambayeque, Peru. I also contributed to a time-series analysis using NDVI, precipitation, and soil moisture to investigate how water availability related to the decline of mesquite forests.

The shared [EO Data Extraction and Processing](../eo-data-extraction-and-processing/) folder includes an original MODIS NDVI HDF extraction script and a reusable GeoTIFF version. This is one example of the data preparation work, not the complete time-series analysis.

Original time-series scripts, datasets, figures, and numerical results have not yet been added. The repository will be expanded as these materials become available.

## Findings from the team poster

The team combined CHIRPS precipitation (1989–2019), GLDAS soil moisture and evapotranspiration (2000–2019), and MODIS/VIIRS vegetation indices (2000–2019) into time-series analyses of the Lambayeque region and its watersheds.

The poster reports decreasing precipitation, stronger vegetation condition during wetter periods, and watershed-dependent differences in soil moisture and evapotranspiration. These are team-level findings, not proof that water availability alone caused mesquite mortality. The presentation notes incomplete dataset coverage, mixed pixels from coarse spatial resolution, incomplete protected-area boundaries, and NDVI observations that did not fully align with information from local partners.
